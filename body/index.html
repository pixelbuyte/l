    What this is, and what it is not

    One skeleton, four duty cycles

    The four bodies share a rig — same joints, in the same places — and differ in what they are specified to do, which is how real product families work. You do not redesign the skeleton; you re-spec it, and the drawing changes because the numbers did.

    The prices are estimates, openly

    Every figure comes from public prices for the same class of part: quasi-direct-drive actuators like the ones open-source humanoids use, harmonic drives where the torque demands them, hobbyist microturbines, an automotive battery pack. Believable, not quoted. A purchasing department would tear the sheet apart — that is what purchasing departments are for.

    The interesting line is the flight group

    Of the whole sheet, more than a third is the ability to fly — four turbines and the plumbing to feed them. That is the honest shape of your idea: the sensor link is nearly free, the hands are dear, and the flying is what decides whether this body costs like a car or like a house.

    Nothing here is buildable from this page

    These are concept elevations, not fabrication drawings. No tolerances, no materials engineering, no control software, and no claim that the mass budget closes — a real airframe team would start by proving it does not. This is the stage before all of that: the stage where you decide which body is worth that work.

    Four elevations, one live parts list, no build step and no dependencies. A companion to A Working Heart — the sensor this body listens to is measuring the thing that page simulates.
